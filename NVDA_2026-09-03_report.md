# NVIDIA Company Research Report

**Company:** NVIDIA Corporation (NASDAQ: NVDA)  
**Research basis:** Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 25, 2026, filed February 25, 2026.  
**Purpose:** Preliminary company research for a reader who has not read the filing. This report is not investment advice. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Cover; filed February 25, 2026]

## Executive summary

NVIDIA is an AI-infrastructure company that sells accelerated-computing platforms: chips, networking, systems, and software used in data centers, as well as graphics products for gaming and professional visualization. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 1—Business, pp. 4–5]

The filing shows exceptional fiscal-2026 growth, led by Data Center demand for Blackwell-based AI computing and networking products. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 36–37, 39–40]

The same filing shows meaningful execution and downside risks: margin pressure from product transition and an H20 charge, reliance on customer infrastructure spending, export-control exposure, customer concentration, and potential inventory or purchase-commitment write-downs. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 36–38, 40–41]

## What the company actually does

NVIDIA operates in two segments: Compute & Networking and Graphics. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 36]

Compute & Networking includes Data Center accelerated-computing and networking platforms, AI solutions and software, and Automotive platforms and autonomous/electric-vehicle software. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 1—Business, p. 4]

Graphics includes GeForce GPUs for gaming PCs and Quadro/NVIDIA RTX GPUs for enterprise workstation graphics. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 1—Business, p. 4]

For Data Center customers, NVIDIA sells more than standalone chips: it offers GPUs, CPUs, interconnects, networking, rack-scale systems, software stacks, libraries, models, APIs, SDKs, and application frameworks. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 1—Business, pp. 4–5]

Its customers include cloud providers, AI model makers, enterprises, startups, public-sector organizations, OEMs, ODMs, system integrators, and distributors. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 1—Business, pp. 4–5; Item 7—MD&A, p. 40]

## What management says changed in fiscal 2026—and why

Revenue rose 65% to $215.9 billion, operating income rose 60% to $130.4 billion, and net income rose 65% to $120.1 billion in fiscal 2026. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 37]

Compute & Networking revenue increased 67% to $193.5 billion, while Graphics revenue increased 57% to $22.5 billion. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 40]

Management attributes the growth primarily to the shift toward accelerated computing and AI: Data Center computing revenue grew 59% on Blackwell demand, and Data Center networking revenue grew 142% on NVLink compute fabric and Ethernet/InfiniBand platform growth. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 40]

Blackwell architectures represented the majority of Data Center revenue in fiscal 2026. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 36]

Gaming revenue rose 41%, Professional Visualization revenue rose 70%, and Automotive revenue rose 39%; management attributes these increases to Blackwell demand, DGX Spark, and adoption of self-driving platforms, respectively. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 37]

Profitability remained high, but gross margin declined from 75.0% to 71.1%. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 37, 39–41]

Management attributes that decline to a transition from Hopper HGX systems to Blackwell full-scale data-center solutions and to a $4.5 billion H20 excess-inventory and purchase-obligation charge. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 36–37, 40–41]

Operating expenses increased 41% to $23.1 billion, driven by employee-related compensation and higher compute and infrastructure costs. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 37, 41]

Operating cash flow rose to $102.7 billion from $64.1 billion, and cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities reached $62.6 billion at fiscal year-end. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 42–43]

## Advantages supported by the filing

NVIDIA’s advantage is its integrated platform rather than a single product: its chips, networking, systems, and CUDA-centered software ecosystem are designed to work together for large-scale AI and high-performance-computing workloads. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 1—Business, pp. 4–5]

The company is participating in several end markets—Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, and Automotive—which broadens its set of applications even though Data Center is the principal growth driver. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 1—Business, p. 4; Item 7—MD&A, pp. 37, 39–40]

Fiscal-2026 growth in both reported segments and operating cash flow indicates that the platform demand translated into reported revenue, operating income, and cash generation during the year. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 37, 40, 42]

## Challenges and risks management highlights

Customer AI deployments require data-center capacity, energy, and capital; management says shortages in any of these could delay deployments, reduce the scale of AI adoption, and hurt future revenue or financial performance. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 36]

NVIDIA is introducing advanced Data Center architectures on a one-year cadence, and management warns that complex product transitions and system configurations can create production delays, supply-demand challenges, quality issues, lower yields, higher costs, warranty costs, and revenue volatility. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 36]

U.S. export restrictions materially affected the H20 product: NVIDIA recorded a $4.5 billion charge after H20 demand diminished, and it reported only about $60 million of H20 revenue under later licenses. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 36]

Management also says it had not generated H200 revenue under its February 2026 China licensing program and did not know whether imports would be allowed. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 36]

Revenue is concentrated: two direct customers represented 22% and 14% of fiscal-2026 revenue, primarily in Compute & Networking. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 40]

Inventory and capacity commitments depend on assumptions about future demand, while some manufacturing lead times extend beyond twelve months; a demand slowdown, technology transition, order deferral, regulation, or stronger competing products could therefore produce inventory or purchase-commitment charges. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 37–38]

NVIDIA’s supply chain was mainly concentrated in Asia at the time of the filing, although the company stated that it was expanding manufacturing activity in the United States and Latin America to improve resiliency. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 36]

Management warns that open-source AI models could reduce demand for NVIDIA products if developers deploy them on competitors’ platforms. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 36]

## Management’s stated outlook

Management does not provide a full-year numerical revenue or earnings forecast in this 10-K MD&A. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 36–43]

It says it will continue a one-year Data Center product cadence and expects Rubin production shipments to begin in the second half of fiscal 2027. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 36; Item 1—Business, p. 5]

Management expects Gaming supply constraints to be a headwind in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 and beyond. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 37]

It also expects fiscal-2027 capital expenditures to exceed fiscal-2026 capital expenditures in support of future growth. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, p. 43]

## Research conclusion

**My call: Watch-defer.**

The filing supports continued research because growth, profitability, and cash generation were strong; however, it does not yet support initiating because gross margin declined, H20/export restrictions produced a large charge, revenue is concentrated, and future demand depends on customers’ infrastructure capacity and capital spending. It is not a do-not-buy conclusion because the reported business performance remains strong. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 36–43]

I would change this call if subsequent filings show that Data Center and Compute & Networking growth remains durable while gross margin and cash conversion stabilize despite export restrictions, customer concentration, and elevated infrastructure commitments. [Source: NVIDIA Form 10-K, Item 7—MD&A, pp. 36–43]
